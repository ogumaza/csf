CSF 1.7.0 tags and plays its rips the way a soundtrack album times its tracks. A rip of a loop gets two passes and the new tags `loop_start` and `loop_length`, and the players play each track from its first sound. Rips of The Wind Waker HD made with 1.7.0 require a player of 1.7.0 or later.

CSF rips Nintendo Wii U sequenced music into a PSF-family format. It plays the music with the sound code of the game, or a model of that code, and with a model of how the console mixes sound. The release includes a ripper (`csfrip`), a renderer (`csfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/csf.md` describes the format.

## New in 1.7.0

- **Lengths as on a soundtrack album.** The `length` tag of a rip now counts from the first sound. A loop gets two passes to the millisecond and a 7-second fade, in place of 10 seconds. It also gets the new tags `loop_start` and `loop_length`: the start of the loop and the time of one pass. A player can read them to play another number of passes. A sound that ends gets `length` at its last sound and a 1-second fade, without the half second of silence that rips had after it. For a JAudio game, the length analysis plays each variant with the tracks that the variant mutes and the volumes that it sets, the way the players play it. `docs/csf.md` describes the tags. Rip a game again to get them.
- **Tracks that play as on a soundtrack album.** The foobar2000 component, the XMPlay plugin and `csfplay` play each track with 0.5 s of silence, then the music from its first sound, then 1 s of silence. Eight new settings of the component and the plugin change this: skipping the silence at the start and at the end, the silences before and after a track, the number of passes of a loop, one pass for long loops, the fade of a loop, and whether the length and fade tags count. `csfplay` has them as `--keep-opening`, `--start-silence`, `--end-silence`, `--loops`, `--one-pass-over`, `--loop-fade`, `--ignore-tags` and `--keep-ending`. `csfplay --raw` renders a file as it is, with no skip and no added silence. The component keeps `loop_start` and `loop_length` when it rewrites the tags of a file. The XMPlay plugin leaves them out of the Message window.
- **A fade that follows the square of the time left.** The fade at the end of a track now follows the square of the time left in it. Most soundtrack albums fade that way. The curve of the fade is a new setting of the component and the plugin. `csfplay --fade-exponent P` sets it from 0.5 to 4. A value of 1 fades linearly.
- **Long loops get both passes.** The length analysis now plays music for up to an hour. A piece whose two passes take longer than ten minutes now gets both. Its rip stopped at ten minutes before. Music means the sounds that `--bgm` selects. Other sounds keep the limit of ten minutes. A sound still playing at either limit gets ten minutes and a fade, with a warning.
- **Compatibility.** Rips of The Wind Waker HD made with 1.7.0 contain a JAudio1 profile of 43 words. CSF 1.6.1 and earlier players accept only 24 or 41 words, and they refuse these rips. Play them with CSF 1.7.0 or later. CSF 1.7.0 still plays the rips of earlier versions. Earlier players ignore the new loop tags.

## Fixed in 1.7.0

- **Music variants of The Wind Waker HD.** A variant that sets a port of a piece, such as `JA_BGM_JI_TRAINING 9=2`, set the port a game frame after the piece started. The piece already reads the port by then. The first moments of such a variant therefore played without its status. The player now sets the ports right after the first tick of the sequence, at the same point in every rip. The profile gains two words for this: the addresses of two functions in the code of the game. Rip the game again to get the fix.

## Other changes

- **The game tag.** The `game` tag is now the value of `--game`, or else the English title in the `meta/meta.xml` of the game. For some games, `csfrip` used the name of the driver profile, a name written in its code. A game without an English title, or whose folder contains the XML files of another title, takes the name of the input. In archive mode, the libraries are named after the game tag too.
- **A DS sound archive.** `csfrip` now names a DS sound archive (SDAT) in its error, as it names the sound archives of the 3DS, the Wii and the Switch: "is a DS sound archive (SDAT); CSF rips Wii U sound archives (big-endian FSAR, .bfsar)". It said that the file wasn't a Wii U game before.
- **The libraries.** CSF now builds on xsflib 1.12.0 and ntdlib 7.1.0.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `csfrip` rips a Wii U game or a sound archive (`.bfsar`) into a `.csflib` and a `.minicsf` for each sound. A game can be a Wii U archive (`.wua`) or an extracted title. `csfrip` requires the files of the game to be stored decrypted. Both forms store them that way. The Title Manager in Cemu converts disc images (WUD and WUX) and installable titles to `.wua` files. `csfrip` finds the group files that a game keeps outside its sound archive. It also looks for them in SARC archives. The ripper plays each sound to work out its length, fade and loop tags. It skips the sounds that make no sound. A rip of The Wind Waker HD contains the sound effects of the game too. An effect that reads a value from the game gets a variant for each value. A piece of music that the game plays in several states gets a file for each state. `--bgm` rips only the music. `csfrip` also supports drag and drop.
- **Two rip modes.** Game mode runs the sound code of the game on an emulated Espresso CPU. The rips play as they do in the game. Game mode requires a driver profile for the game. Four games have one: Animal Crossing: amiibo Festival (USA) and Mario Party 10 (USA), on the NintendoWare sound library, The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess HD (USA), on JAudio2, and The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker HD (USA and Japan), on JAudio1. Mario Party 10 is ripped in archive mode unless `--mode game` selects game mode. Archive mode plays a sound archive from any game with a model of the sound library in the SDK. The model follows the version of the library in amiibo Festival.
- **Mixing.** The Wii U mixes its voices on a DSP. The DSP program comes with the system, not with the game. The sound library of the system then mixes the result for each output. In both modes, CSF does both stages in code: the resampling, filters, envelopes and volumes of the voices, and the final mix stage with its upsampling to 48 kHz. The code does the same arithmetic as the DSP program. A JAudio game mixes its voices on the CPU instead. The player runs the mixer of the game before the final mix stage.
- **Effects.** For a game with an effect profile, archive mode plays the reverb, chorus and delay that the game sets up. Four games have one: Animal Crossing: amiibo Festival, Mario Party 10, Nintendo Land (all USA) and Wii Party U (USA, Rev 1).
- **Rendering.** `csfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at 48,000 Hz, the output rate of the Wii U.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.minicsf` files using their length, fade and loop tags. Each track plays from its first sound, with a short silence before and after it. Tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback are supported. A separate thread renders ahead of playback and keeps up to ten minutes of audio. That makes seeking within a track quick.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.minicsf` files using their length, fade and loop tags. Each track plays from its first sound, with a short silence before and after it. Endless playback is supported. Like the component, the plugin renders ahead of playback and keeps up to ten minutes of audio.

## Known limitations

- Game mode has profiles for four games. Other games with the NintendoWare sound library rip in archive mode. The model in archive mode follows the version of the sound library in amiibo Festival. A game with another version may play differently from the console. A JAudio game without a profile can't be ripped.
- The DSP program reads the coefficients of its resampling filters from the ROM of the DSP. No file contains that ROM. CSF uses the coefficients of the Wii. They're very likely the same.
- The code of a game can set up the effects of the sound library in the console (reverb, chorus and delay). Archive mode plays them only for a game with an effect profile. Four games have one. A sound archive ripped on its own plays without them. Game mode plays without effects.
- The output is the stereo mix for the TV. The GamePad output, the speakers of the Wii Remotes and the surround mix aren't played.
- A sound that depends on values the game sets plays as it does before the game sets any, unless a profile gives it variants. A variant plays its state from the start. The game moves a piece of music into a new state over up to a few seconds. In Twilight Princess HD, the code of some fights writes statuses once the piece plays. A rip leaves those states out. A sequence that draws random numbers takes the same path every time.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them (such as ambiences) get two loops and a fade if the ripper can detect their loops. Otherwise, they play until their notes end. The analysis plays music for up to an hour and other sounds for up to ten minutes. A sound still playing at the limit gets ten minutes and a fade.
- Only sequenced sounds are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BFSTM streams. Wave sounds aren't ripped. Sequences whose data is in a group file that can't be found are skipped. The Wind Waker HD plays some of its music from streams. A rip of the game contains the music that the game plays as sequences. It also contains the sound effects of the game. JAudio plays those as sequences too. Four effects of the game make no sound in a rip with any value. `csfrip` skips them.
- A rip of a JAudio game runs the mixer of the game on the emulated CPU. It renders far more slowly than other rips. A slow computer may not play it in real time.
- Game mode runs the sound code of the game on an emulated CPU. The 32-bit XMPlay plugin plays game-mode rips at about real time with nothing to spare, and it can stutter on a slower or busy PC. Use the 64-bit foobar2000 component for game-mode rips.
- A seek back past the audio that the component or the XMPlay plugin keeps renders from the start of the track. Such seeks happen only in tracks longer than ten minutes and in endless playback.
- Encrypted games (disc images and installable titles) aren't read.
